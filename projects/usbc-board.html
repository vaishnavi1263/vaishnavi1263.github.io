USB-C ESP32-S3 Development Board

A battery-capable ESP32-S3 board, powered and programmed over a single USB-C cable. The design is complete on paper: requirements, component choices, power budget, pin plan, PCB rules and bring-up plan. Schematic capture in KiCad is next; the board has not been made yet.

  • ESP32-S3
  • USB-C
  • Li-ion charging
  • Buck regulator
  • ESD protection
  • KiCad

Key design decisions

  • 5.1 kΩ CC resistors: without them, a USB-C charger supplies 0 V. This is the most common USB-C board mistake.
  • Native USB-Serial/JTAG: no USB-to-serial chip; flashing, the serial console and debugging all go over one cable.
  • Buck regulator instead of an LDO: an LDO from 5 V would dissipate about 0.5 W at Wi-Fi load and heat up by more than 100 °C.
  • 250 mA charge current: keeps the typical draw within the 500 mA a laptop USB port guarantees.
  • Pin plan: SPI on the direct-routed pins, battery sensing on ADC1 because ADC2 doesn't work during Wi-Fi, and strapping pins kept off the headers.
  • Bring-up plan: 8 steps, each with a pass criterion, with the bench supply current-limited throughout.
